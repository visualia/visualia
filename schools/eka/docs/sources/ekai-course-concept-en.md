## Goal of the course

To provide a curious and fearless introduction to AI in creative context, taking advantage of latest advancements in LLM models and coding capabilities, coupling it with existing EKA resources: people, places, courses and research. In addition to practical, "correct" LLM learning we need to stay critical, aware of ethics, cultural bias, and ecological footprint, staying with the glitches, misreads, cultural weirdness, and see the power structures behind them. AI should be something you try, stage, relate, challenge, and sometimes just say no to.

## Possible themes

### Coming back to our senses
AI-based user interfaces needs sensory-rich experiences that engage multiple senses. Tools should balance clarity with openness, leaving space for exploration, meaning, and co-creation. Digital environments should act as mirrors for thought, respecting human rhythms of learning to make education more deeply human.

- https://wattenberger.com/thoughts/our-interfaces-have-lost-their-senses
- https://wattenberger.com/thoughts/hard-and-soft

This theme could explore the rich input AI can actually consume, use as much of sensors / signal we can provide to the system to detect meaning and create the (even unexpected) experiences and convert digital back to the analog artifacts.

This theme can also be pushed toward AI + body-centered experiences (see Taavet Jansen / Liis Vares) or the loneliness and AI surveillance angle based on "Love, Loneliness, and AI — Where Should We Begin, Live with Esther Perel and Spike Jonze".

### Hardware controlled by AI software
The theme should explore both the possibilities and risks regarding AI operating in and affecting real world by writing software for real hardware. Experiments can start with classic breadboard+microcontroller prototypes but with AI-written and AI-debugged software layer. Rene Rebane can lead such workshops.

The experiments can also use speculative design methods to explore and prototype both utopias and dystopias when "world is built on software" thesis becomes "world is built on AI software".

Theme can also be pushed towards AI-built / managed architecture https://a16z.com/every-building-youve-ever-been-in-was-designed-by-software-built-in-1997/ or the self-defense / vulnerability angle (see Silver Keskküla).

### Creative hallucinations
As multimodal AI is getting more and more polished, visually perfect and real-life-like there might be an opportunity to use either LLM models with minimal training sets or models from the past that can not generate the visual quality of the big commercial models… but can generate something else, something more creative and edgy (see the EKI LLM leaderboard). This theme also allows students to understand the capability gap between the different models and models resource usage in general.

This theme can be pushed towards "local models" / "open source models" / "build your own models" / AI-critical activism track.

### AI doing the dishes
"I want AI to do my laundry and dishes so that I can do art and writing, not for AI to do my art and writing so that I can do my laundry and dishes." https://medium.com/@dsaidinesh2003/let-ai-do-the-dishes-not-your-writing-fc499de63e1b

Coupled with "I Tried the First Humanoid Home Robot. It Got Weird. | WSJ" we question the AI impact in our physical world and societal lives: what happens in job market when white collar jobs (40% of workforce) really are going away https://arvamus.delfi.ee/artikkel/120441760/kolumn-tonu-runnel-pane-vaim-valmis-ka-sina-kaotad-too

The course can model some of these scenarios, invent the new jobs and picture the society in the wake of another automation revolution.

### AI footprint visualization
AI has a huge cost: economical, societal, environmental. To build awareness and critical thinking around that, we can use AI itself to visualize its actual impact, do the real life comparisons and realtime tracking of the experiment cost (not just monetary cost) of all we do during the course. https://github.com/mlco2/codecarbon

Theme can be pushed towards AI-based data visualization (https://claude.com/blog/claude-builds-visuals, https://www.napkin.ai/) or "AI stomping on the small cultures", see EKI research on LLM performance regarding Estonian culture knowledge.
