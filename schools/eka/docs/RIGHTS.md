# Rights: what EKA's students may use

Draft 2026-11-02, from a claude.ai chat (24 October to 2 November). Which
historical material is free to use, train on and publish, and which
exceptions EKA has. Not legal advice: confirm with EKA's legal team.

## Prior art and rights

### Bauhaus (EU and Estonia: 70 years after the author's death)

| Status | Who (death year) |
|---|---|
| Public domain | Klee (1940), Schlemmer (1943), Kandinsky (1944), Moholy-Nagy (1946), Joost Schmidt (1948) |
| From 1 Jan 2027 | Feininger (1956) |
| Not free | Itten (1967), Gropius (1969), Albers (1976), Breuer (1981), Stölzl and Brandt (1983), Bayer (1985), Lucia Moholy (1989) |

- Texts: the Bauhaus books by Klee (*Pedagogical Sketchbook*), Kandinsky
  (*Point and Line to Plane*) and Moholy-Nagy are free in German;
  translations may be protected. Bibliothèque Kandinsky digitised Bauhaus
  writings.
- In Estonia, reproductions of public-domain works are outside the Copyright
  Act's protection (flat photos of 2D works). Photos of 3D objects can carry
  the photographer's rights.
- US sources (MoMA) mark these works as protected, since US terms differ.

### Estonian pre-war (1918–1940)

| Status | Who (death year) |
|---|---|
| Public domain | Köler (1899), Kallis (1918), Mägi (1925), Koort (1935), Triik (1940), Laikmaa (1942), Kristjan Raud (1943, incl. *Kalevipoeg* illustrations), Wiiralt (1954, free since 2025) |
| Not yet | Ado Vabbe (1961 → 2032), Günther Reindorff (1974 → 2045; much interwar state graphics) |
| Probably free | anonymous pre-war ephemera (ads, packaging, labels, unsigned posters): 70 years after publication; check each item |
| Folk patterns | the traditional patterns themselves are free |

Death years are from general knowledge; verify each before publishing.

### Sources to harvest

- **DIGAR (National Library):** per-item rights ("Copyrighted until …"), so
  items can be filtered by date. Data mining of restricted content only in the
  library, for scientific purposes.
- **MuIS:** 59 museums, millions of objects, open data at opendata.muis.ee;
  licences vary by museum. Wikidata holds MuIS identifiers, so "artists who
  died before 1956 with objects in MuIS" can be queried.
- To check: Ajapaik, the National Archives' photo database, the Estonian
  National Museum, the Estonian Folklore Archives, Wikimedia Eesti.
- Neighbours: Finnish Heritage Agency (100,000+ images, mostly CC BY), Swedish
  History Museum (CC BY, Public Domain Mark for expired works).
- International: Harvard Bauhaus Special Collection (32,000+ objects, IIIF;
  rights-restricted images excluded; educational and non-commercial use
  encouraged), The Met Open Access (CC0, 400,000+ images), Europeana, Internet
  Archive, Biodiversity Heritage Library, Wikimedia Commons.

### Exceptions available to EKA

- **Teaching:** Estonia kept its unremunerated education exception and added
  the DSM Article 5 digital teaching exception.
- **Text and data mining:** Article 3 (research organisations and cultural
  heritage institutions, scientific research, no opt-out) and Article 4
  (everyone, unless rights holders opt out); Estonia's implementation follows
  the Directive (§ 19² of the Copyright Act).
- **Catch:** exceptions cover class analysis and teaching, not public
  publication. Public works (projects.visualia.space) use public-domain or CC0
  material only. Confirm with EKA's legal team.

### Three levels

1. **Free** (public domain, CC0): anything, including published work and
   training data.
2. **Semi-free** (exceptions, museums' non-commercial terms): class analysis,
   taste features, internal Judgement Machines; no republishing.
3. **Not free** (living or recently deceased designers): link and discuss.
