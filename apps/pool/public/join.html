What this does

  • Your browser downloads the AI model you chose (once) and runs it on your computer’s graphics chip with WebLLM. One model at a time; switching unloads the other.
  • While you're in the pool, people who ask for that model (pool/qwen2.5-1.5b, pool/qwen3-4b or pool/qwen3.5-9b) get their answer written here, and the words go back to them as they are written.
  • Each job shows in the list above while it runs: the model, how many tokens, how many seconds.
  • Every job is also on the class usage page as served by you, at no cost.
  • One job at a time. If you stop or close the tab in the middle of one, the cloud finishes it.

What it never does

  • It never opens your files, runs programs, or installs anything. A job is only “write text for this prompt”.
  • It doesn’t show or keep the prompts. They pass through this tab’s memory to the model and are gone.
  • It doesn’t tell you who asked, and it doesn’t tell them it was you (the class usage page does, as a pseudonymous id).
  • The model doesn’t learn from prompts; it is a fixed file.
  • Nothing runs when this tab is closed. No background service, nothing left running.
  • Your computer accepts no incoming connections. This tab connects out, to one address.

The addresses it talks to

pool.visualia.space
this page, sign-in, and the job connection (one outgoing WebSocket)
cdn.jsdelivr.net
the WebLLM program, version 0.2.86
huggingface.co, *.hf.co
the model files, downloaded once
raw.githubusercontent.com
the model’s graphics code (mlc-ai/binary-mlc-llm-libs)

The browser blocks every other address for this page (its Content Security Policy).

What it stores, and how to stop

  • The model files, in this browser’s storage for pool.visualia.space, and a sign-in cookie. Nothing else.
