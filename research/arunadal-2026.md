## The event

Arunädal (18.–22.11.2026), the creative higher education conference *"Loovus ja tehisaru"* at
EKA on **20.11**, free, registration on Fienta (https://www.eesti.ai/arunadal2026). Three
themes: creative education, authorship, and **väärtustriiv**, how AI biases shape cultural
attitudes. Also on the day: Ingolf Wunder, Raivo Kelomees. The page doesn't list the speakers
of this block; that it is this event is an inference from the theme and the place.

## The speakers

- **Katrin Laas-Mikko**: AI ethicist at the University of Tartu Centre for Ethics, head of risk
  and compliance at SK ID Solutions, PhD on moral risks of new technology. Co-author of the
  **TI-Hüpe risk analysis** (https://tihupe.ee/en/risk-assessment/, PDF
  https://tihupe.ee/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/TI-Huppe-riskide-analuus-august-2026.pdf).
  There, value drift is model answers shaped by training data, developer choices, safety
  filters, content policies and commercial aims; alienation is students treating AI as a
  person and drifting from social contact. Expect risks 6 (Estonian culture) and 7 (value
  drift) from `DRIFT.md`.
- **Kurmo Konsa**: associate professor of archival studies (Tartu), professor of conservation
  (Tartu Art College); heritage, information ecology, keeping digital culture
  (https://www.err.ee/1608344396/kurmo-konsa-digikultuuri-tulevikule-sailitamisest). On
  language models (ERR, 5.2.2024, https://www.err.ee/1609241403/kurmo-konsa-kas-juturobot-motleb):
  good models of language, far from good models of human thinking. A search summary also
  credits him with AI as a sensitive mirror of human culture, carrying our prejudices and
  values; source not found, check before quoting.
- **Gregor Taul**: art historian and semiotician at EKA (https://artun.ee/inimesed/gregor-taul),
  PhD on late Soviet monumental art; writes for Sirp and Vikerkaar, also on art in schools.
  *"Tõejões"* (Vikerkaar, January 2024, https://gregortaul.ee/textsinest/toejoes): culture
  made a commodity, AI that knows everything yet is foolish and polarising, corporate
  networks. Likely to steer the panel to culture and power rather than technology.
- **Lennart Mänd**: EKA professor, head of leather art since 2008, book art
  (https://www.ekabl.ee/id/mandlennart). Closing words, probably in an EKA role.

## How the demo fits

The demo opens the block, so it is the one concrete thing the two talks and the panel can
point back to. Laas-Mikko gives the frame (TI-Hüpe risks 6 and 7), Konsa asks what is lost
when everything can be recreated; the demo shows the bias happening before either.

DRIFT.md's addition is the counterweight to the sovereignty framing: drift is also in what AI
*makes*, not only in foreign providers, and our own tools drift too.
