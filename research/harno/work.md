# IT, AI and work

Part of `README.md`. Gathered 2026-11-11. *Speculation* and *from memory* mark what was not checked.

## IT, AI and work: Estonia

- **Pipedrive**, 29.10.2026: 267 jobs cut in Estonia, 70 % of local staff (219 Tallinn,
  48 Tartu): analysts, designers, marketing. The CEO's stated reason: AI changed how
  software is discovered, free web traffic fell sharply, paid traffic costs more. They
  lost their audience, not their coders
  (https://news.err.ee/1610180296/estonian-unicorn-pipedrive-announces-major-restructuring-office-closures).
- **Telia**: 100 jobs. The state is merging IT houses (RIT into RIA, then RIKS).
- **OSKA** (via https://www.byteseu.com/2488598/, 31.10.2026): IT employment among
  under-25s fell by nearly a quarter in three years. Urve Mets (OSKA): *"it is considerably
  more difficult for young people to enter the labor market today than it perhaps was
  three years ago."* Telia CEO Andre Visse: *"we will be able to do the same work with
  increasingly fewer people."* Sergei Anikin (ex-Pipedrive engineering): *"no stupid idea
  will go unrealized, so what will actually become important is the ability to
  distinguish stupid ideas from good ones."*
- **OSKA's older IT forecast**: 1.5× IT specialists in seven years, about 2,600 new a year.
  Written before agents.
- **OSKA AI study**, Nov 2025 (https://uuringud.oska.kutsekoda.ee/uuringud/ai-uuring):
  firms using AI 14 % (2024) to 22 % (2025). Main barrier is skills. Three roles: end
  users, solution "smart buyers", leaders. Estonia builds custom solutions well and
  chooses off-the-shelf ones badly.
- **Arenguseire Keskus**, June 2026
  (https://arenguseire.ee/uudised/raport-eestis-voib-tehisaru-kasutus-jaada-pinnapealseks-ega-too-oodatud-tootlikkuse-kasvu/):
  automation alone +6–9 % productivity; with organisational change +19 % in
  manufacturing, +23 % in knowledge services; combined social, cognitive and technical
  skills pay about 6 % more. Priit Vahter: *"Automated firms need social and
  collaboration skills."*
- **Lauri Haav** (//koodi): Estonia needs 3,400–7,100 more ICT people outside the IT sector
  to reach the EU average
  (https://www.err.ee/1610185624/lauri-haav-it-sektori-kasutamata-voimalus-peitub-teistes-majandusharudes).
- **Digiühiskonna arengukava 2035**: by 2030, three quarters of firms and the public
  sector use AI; more sovereign compute.

## IT, AI and work: wider

- Stanford "canaries" study: entry-level software developer jobs down nearly 20 % since
  2022; ages 22–25 in highly AI-exposed jobs about 19 % below peers in less exposed ones.
  Where AI automates, junior hiring falls; where it augments, employment holds.
- WEF Future of Jobs: 170 M roles created, 92 M displaced by 2030; 39 % of skills
  change. McKinsey scenario for Estonia: 13 % of jobs gone, about 15 % created, net +1.6 %.
  Moods, not measurements.
- Gallup and Lumina: 47 % of US college students have considered switching majors
  because of AI.
- **The Stanford chart, as a slide** (screenshots from a video the owner watched, speaker
  not named; `sources/img/stanford-canaries-2026-08-*.png`). Brynjolfsson, Chandar and
  Chen, *"No Widespread Displacement, but the AI Employment Gap for Young Workers Has
  Widened to 19%"*, Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 12.8.2026, highlights of the revised
  *"Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts About the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial
  Intelligence"*. Chart: headcount of ages 22–25 by AI exposure, ADP payroll data,
  normalised to 1.0 at late 2022 (ChatGPT). By mid-2026 the least exposed groups sit at
  about 1.08–1.14, the most exposed at about 0.88, overall about 0.98 (read off the
  picture). **Read 2026-11-12** (https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/,
  through a fetch summary): ADP payroll, November 2022 to June 2026, exposure quintiles
  after Eloundou et al. (2024). The gap was 15 % at the July 2025 vintage, 19 % now.
  - *"Declines are concentrated in occupations where AI usage tends to automate human
    tasks. In occupations where AI is used more to complement workers, employment is
    flat or rising."*
  - *"The adjustment appears to operate primarily through reduced hiring of young
    workers rather than increased separations."* *"Experienced workers show no
    comparable gap."* So far employment moves, base pay does not.
  - *"We do not see widespread, economy-wide job displacement associated with AI."*
  - Holds without tech firms, with interest rates and remote work controlled, and on
    other exposure measures.
  - The authors' caveats: *"These are descriptive patterns, not causal estimates."* The
    gap shrinks once education is accounted for, some trends predate generative AI, and
    the ADP sample shows larger effects than national surveys.
  - For the talk: the young are not out of work overall, they are not being let in to
    the jobs AI does. The door closes, nobody is pushed out.
- **Lenny Rachitsky, *State of the tech job market: late 2026*** (10.11.2026,
  https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/state-of-the-tech-job-market-late; TrueUp openings at
  9,000+ firms, Live Data Technologies US hires). Mostly US. The pattern: fewer people
  hired to make things, more hired to decide what gets made.
  - **Making roles shrink.** US software engineer hires about 113,000 in the last 12
    months against 239,000 in 2022, down every year since. Openings +44 % but hires
    −17 % since January 2024. Mobile and front-end demand nearly halved in a year.
    Product designer hires 12,600 against 27,600 in 2022; about 1,900 open design roles
    worldwide, the lowest on record. User researchers 1,800 hires against 5,300 (−66 %),
    108 open jobs worldwide.
  - **Deciding roles grow.** Open product manager roles at a near four-year high, +75 %
    from the March 2023 low; PM to design openings 3.8 : 1 (1.9 in early 2023).
    Forward-deployed engineers (building with the customer, on site) 4.6× in a year,
    about 1,500 open.
  - **The tools are the skills now.** "LLM" is the most-mentioned skill in PM postings,
    "Claude" third, "evals" entering the top 10; for engineers "LLM" just passed Java;
    for designers "Claude" passed CSS in spring 2026.
  - **Place matters again.** 15 % of open tech roles in the Bay Area (record), remote
    14 % (21 % in late 2022). More Bay Area PM, engineering and design openings than
    remote ones worldwide. Bad news for a small country that hired remote.
  - Nothing on juniors by age in the free part; the closing section ("growing, but
    outside of software roles") is paywalled.
  - Fits Anikin's line above: telling good ideas from stupid ones is the job that grows.
- **"AI rebounding"**: Joshua Hong, *'AI rebounding': Nearly 20% of U.S. workers replaced
  by AI are being asked back*, Fortune, 9.11.2026
  (https://fortune.com/2026/11/09/ai-rebounding-20-us-workers-replaced-higher-pay-job-security),
  read 2026-11-12 through a fetch summary.
  - SHL (a talent assessment firm, so it sells the remedy) surveyed in June and July,
    sample size not given: 19.1 % of US respondents say they were replaced by AI and
    then asked back, 16 % across the US and UK, **24 % of those aged 18–24**. Of those
    asked back, 51 % wanted a substantial raise, 42 % written job security, 40 %
    openness about AI at work.
  - Robert Half, July: 32 % of US hiring managers whose firms cut roles for AI later
    reinstated some. Reasons: institutional knowledge or context AI could not replace
    (40 %), relationships (39 %), more demand (39 %), more human oversight and quality
    control than expected (38 %).
  - Klarna took back human support agents *"for complex, sensitive cases"*; Ford hired
    350 engineers in June after AI quality control tools *"failed to perform"*.
  - Challenger, Gray & Christmas: AI named in 121,401 announced US job cuts through
    October 2026, more than double the 54,836 of all 2025.
  - Lucy Beaumont (SHL): *"AI rebounding is what happens when a company automates a role
    before it has genuinely assessed the human capability inside it."*
  - For the talk: what comes back is context, relationships and judgement, the things a
    junior learns only by being there. Self-reported surveys by firms with a stake in
    the answer, so weaker evidence than Stanford's payroll data.
- **The optimist's side, for balance:** Dario Amodei, *Machines of Loving Grace*
  (October 2024, https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace), shown in the same
  video: *"I think that most people are underestimating just how radical the upside of AI
  could be, just as I think most people are underestimating how bad the risks could
  be."* Screenshot `sources/img/amodei-machines-of-loving-grace.png`. **Read
  2026-11-12** (through a fetch summary):
  - Five areas: biology and health, neuroscience and mental health, economic
    development, peace and governance, work and meaning. Powerful AI as *"a country of
    geniuses in a datacenter"*; 50 to 100 years of biology compressed into 5 to 10.
  - On work he is least sure: *"The economic piece actually seems more difficult to me
    than the meaning piece."* Comparative advantage keeps people employed for a while,
    but *"in the long run AI will become so broadly effective and so cheap that this
    will no longer apply"*, and what follows is *"something no one today has done a
    good job of envisioning."*
  - On meaning, the passage closest to the talk: *"it is very likely a mistake to
    believe that tasks you undertake are meaningless simply because an AI could do them
    better."* *"Most people are not the best in the world at anything, and it doesn't
    seem to bother them particularly much."* Meaning comes from *"human relationships
    and connection, not from economic labor."*
  - His own caveat: *"I don't know if this world is realistic, and even if it is, it
    will not be achieved without a huge amount of effort and struggle."*
  - For the talk: the closing question (*what do we still need one another for?*)
    answered by the person who sells the tools, in the talk's own direction.

## Checked and qualified figures (third round)

- **OSKA under-25 figure, confirmed in reporting:** ICT specialists under 25 fell from
  2,405 (2022) to 1,839 (2025), about 24 %. In the EU 52 % of ICT specialists work
  outside the tech sector, in Estonia 42 %
  (https://www.err.ee/1610185624/lauri-haav-it-sektori-kasutamata-voimalus-peitub-teistes-majandusharudes).
  The source table is in the OSKA IKT monitoring report 2025
  (https://uuringud.oska.kutsekoda.ee/uuringud/ikt-seire). It says nothing about how much
  AI caused.
- **Stanford's August 2026 update:** a 19 % *relative* shortfall for ages 22–25 in highly
  AI-exposed jobs, not a 19 % fall in junior jobs, and not proof of cause.
- **IZA 2026:** a 14–15 % relative fall in junior versus senior developer vacancies
  (paper not located).

## The broken apprenticeship

- **The broken apprenticeship.** Beginners learn by doing imperfect work, getting
  criticism and gradually taking responsibility. If organisations automate the juniors'
  work, where do future experts come from? Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension* (1966); Schön,
  *The Reflective Practitioner* (1983); Lave and Wenger, *Situated Learning* (1991).
  Don't hand this structural problem to schools.
- The tension in one line: *"If we no longer need beginners to do the work, how will
  beginners ever become experts?"* Employers, universities and policymakers own it, not
  schools; creativity alone does not guarantee employability.
- Play and curiosity help people notice, persist and revise, but cannot replace paid
  apprenticeships, mentorship or institutional responsibility.
- **Kristjan Port says it himself** (interview, *Postimees Nädal*, 2026-11-14, pp. 16-17,
  read in print): Estonia's youth unemployment is fifth highest in the EU and entry to work
  is getting harder because of AI. A rational manager sees that newcomers are expensive
  while AI does the same work about 80 % cheaper, 10-20 % better and faster: *"See ei ole
  sotsiaalhooldus, see on kapitalism."* So the entry-route question is no longer only the
  speaker's extension of his five-lawyers column (`sources/keynote-brief.md`).
- Same interview, the wider picture: this time the exposed are well-paid, educated IT
  specialists building the automation (*"inimesed saevad iseenda oksa"*); after IT comes
  any work where data goes in and a decision comes out (law, healthcare, accounting,
  logistics). On the WEF forecast (92 million roles gone, 170 million new): more roles does
  not mean a person fills them, and nobody goes to school to earn a low wage. Work that
  stays: social care, and shaping our environment, where human creativity still counts.

## Predictions

- **Anikin's line** as a prediction: when building is free, the job is choosing. Same as
  OSKA's "smart buyer".
- **Jevons for software** (speculation): cheaper software, far more of it; demand moves
  to people who know what to build and for whom.
- **Audience collapses into trust** (speculation from Pipedrive): Kevin Kelly, *"1,000
  True Fans"* (2008), local reputation, communities.
- **IT careers look like artists' careers** (speculation): commissions, residencies,
  workshops, portfolios. Freedom and precarity together.
- **Small language** (speculation): everything the big models get wrong about Estonia is
  local work for young makers. The EKA visuals tests show how little models know.
