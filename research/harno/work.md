# IT, AI and work

Part of `README.md`. Gathered 2026-11-11. *Speculation* and *from memory* mark what was not checked.

## IT, AI and work: Estonia

- **Pipedrive**, 29.10.2026: 267 jobs cut in Estonia, 70 % of local staff (219 Tallinn,
  48 Tartu): analysts, designers, marketing. The CEO's stated reason: AI changed how
  software is discovered, free web traffic fell sharply, paid traffic costs more. They
  lost their audience, not their coders
  (https://news.err.ee/1610180296/estonian-unicorn-pipedrive-announces-major-restructuring-office-closures).
- **Telia**: 100 jobs. The state is merging IT houses (RIT into RIA, then RIKS).
- **OSKA** (via https://www.byteseu.com/2488598/, 31.10.2026): IT employment among
  under-25s fell by nearly a quarter in three years. Urve Mets (OSKA): *"it is considerably
  more difficult for young people to enter the labor market today than it perhaps was
  three years ago."* Telia CEO Andre Visse: *"we will be able to do the same work with
  increasingly fewer people."* Sergei Anikin (ex-Pipedrive engineering): *"no stupid idea
  will go unrealized, so what will actually become important is the ability to
  distinguish stupid ideas from good ones."*
- **OSKA's older IT forecast**: 1.5× IT specialists in seven years, about 2,600 new a year.
  Written before agents.
- **OSKA AI study**, Nov 2025 (https://uuringud.oska.kutsekoda.ee/uuringud/ai-uuring):
  firms using AI 14 % (2024) to 22 % (2025). Main barrier is skills. Three roles: end
  users, solution "smart buyers", leaders. Estonia builds custom solutions well and
  chooses off-the-shelf ones badly.
- **Arenguseire Keskus**, June 2026
  (https://arenguseire.ee/uudised/raport-eestis-voib-tehisaru-kasutus-jaada-pinnapealseks-ega-too-oodatud-tootlikkuse-kasvu/):
  automation alone +6–9 % productivity; with organisational change +19 % in
  manufacturing, +23 % in knowledge services; combined social, cognitive and technical
  skills pay about 6 % more. Priit Vahter: *"Automated firms need social and
  collaboration skills."*
- **Lauri Haav** (//koodi): Estonia needs 3,400–7,100 more ICT people outside the IT sector
  to reach the EU average
  (https://www.err.ee/1610185624/lauri-haav-it-sektori-kasutamata-voimalus-peitub-teistes-majandusharudes).
- **Digiühiskonna arengukava 2035**: by 2030, three quarters of firms and the public
  sector use AI; more sovereign compute.

## IT, AI and work: wider

- Stanford "canaries" study: entry-level software developer jobs down nearly 20 % since
  2022; ages 22–25 in highly AI-exposed jobs about 19 % below peers in less exposed ones.
  Where AI automates, junior hiring falls; where it augments, employment holds.
- WEF Future of Jobs: 170 M roles created, 92 M displaced by 2030; 39 % of skills
  change. McKinsey scenario for Estonia: 13 % of jobs gone, about 15 % created, net +1.6 %.
  Moods, not measurements.
- Gallup and Lumina: 47 % of US college students have considered switching majors
  because of AI.

## Checked and qualified figures (third round)

- **OSKA under-25 figure, confirmed in reporting:** ICT specialists under 25 fell from
  2,405 (2022) to 1,839 (2025), about 24 %. In the EU 52 % of ICT specialists work
  outside the tech sector, in Estonia 42 %
  (https://www.err.ee/1610185624/lauri-haav-it-sektori-kasutamata-voimalus-peitub-teistes-majandusharudes).
  The source table is in the OSKA IKT monitoring report 2025
  (https://uuringud.oska.kutsekoda.ee/uuringud/ikt-seire). It says nothing about how much
  AI caused.
- **Stanford's August 2026 update:** a 19 % *relative* shortfall for ages 22–25 in highly
  AI-exposed jobs, not a 19 % fall in junior jobs, and not proof of cause.
- **IZA 2026:** a 14–15 % relative fall in junior versus senior developer vacancies
  (paper not located).

## The broken apprenticeship

- **The broken apprenticeship.** Beginners learn by doing imperfect work, getting
  criticism and gradually taking responsibility. If organisations automate the juniors'
  work, where do future experts come from? Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension* (1966); Schön,
  *The Reflective Practitioner* (1983); Lave and Wenger, *Situated Learning* (1991).
  Don't hand this structural problem to schools.
- The tension in one line: *"If we no longer need beginners to do the work, how will
  beginners ever become experts?"* Employers, universities and policymakers own it, not
  schools; creativity alone does not guarantee employability.
- Play and curiosity help people notice, persist and revise, but cannot replace paid
  apprenticeships, mentorship or institutional responsibility.

## Predictions

- **Anikin's line** as a prediction: when building is free, the job is choosing. Same as
  OSKA's "smart buyer".
- **Jevons for software** (speculation): cheaper software, far more of it; demand moves
  to people who know what to build and for whom.
- **Audience collapses into trust** (speculation from Pipedrive): Kevin Kelly, *"1,000
  True Fans"* (2008), local reputation, communities.
- **IT careers look like artists' careers** (speculation): commissions, residencies,
  workshops, portfolios. Freedom and precarity together.
- **Small language** (speculation): everything the big models get wrong about Estonia is
  local work for young makers. The EKA visuals tests show how little models know.
