# AI and learning

Part of `README.md`. Gathered 2026-11-11. *Speculation* and *from memory* mark what was not checked.

The distinction that runs through all of it: **performance versus learning**. A polished
answer, generated lesson or working program does not establish durable understanding.
Teachers already know this.

## Studies

- OECD *Digital Education Outlook 2026*: general-purpose AI can lift immediate task
  performance without lasting learning; pedagogically designed systems do better.
- A randomised university trial (2,379 students), with negative grade and engagement
  estimates in some comparisons: *"The Effects of Course-Integrated AI Tutoring on
  Student Performance and Engagement"*, https://edworkingpapers.com/ai26-1598
- Tutoring-style AI use gave more durable learning than delegating the writing:
  *"Experimental Evidence on the Learning Impact of Generative AI"*,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849 (preprint).
- Structured AI tutoring beat unrestricted use, and voice didn't help: *"When AI Tutors
  Speak"*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23958 (small study).
- UNESCO GEM Report 2023, *"Technology in education: a tool on whose terms?"*

These differ in populations, interventions and outcomes; they don't add up to "AI helps"
or "AI harms". The useful question is what the learner goes on thinking, doing, testing
and understanding.

## Estonia

- **TI-Hüpe** (AI Leap) first year 2025/26: 154 of 156 upper secondary schools, about 5,000
  teachers and 20,000 pupils; 94 % of teachers used AI, 63 % changed their teaching; weekly
  pupil use of the ITI app fell from 47 % (March) to 20 % (May). Tartu, Stanford and OpenAI
  impact study, 9,000 pupils, results late 2026
  (https://tihupe.ee/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/AI-Leap-First-Annual-Review-202526.pdf).
- **TI-Hüpe risk analysis** (Tartu ethics centre, 2026): risk 6 Estonian culture, risk 7
  value drift via an external provider, risk 12 a domestic solution of lower quality.
  More in `~/personal/visualia/schools/eka/docs/DRIFT.md`.
- Bardone and Forsler, *"The Estonian AI leap: navigating uncertainty through the
  production of education futures"*, *Learning, Media and Technology*, 2026
  (doi:10.1080/17439884.2026.2720813; the DOI resolves).
- TI-Hüpe researchers Jaan Aru, Kristjan-Julius Laak and Grete Arro on whether the app
  helps learning.
- Tallinn University AI Learning Lab, 2026–2028 (https://www.tlu.ee/en/ai-learning-lab).
- The Tartu ethics centre risk analysis
  (https://eetikakeskus.ut.ee/en/news/centre-ethics-university-tartu-analysis-highlights-major-risks-ai-education).
- Adoption is not change: in TI-Hüpe, the 63 % who changed their teaching says more than
  the 94 % who used AI, and self-reported change is not evidence of learning.
- TI-Hüpe pages to read in full: research and reviews (https://tihupe.ee/en/research-and-reviews/),
  especially pupils' perspectives and the teacher survey; and the researchers on whether
  the app helps (https://tihupe.ee/en/researchers-how-do-we-know-that-the-ai-leap-learning-app-actually-helps-students-learn/).
- Leads to check: ProgeTiiger and õpilasfirmad for pupils making things; Mart Laanpere,
  Piret Luik, and at the TLÜ lab Inge Timoštšuk and Kaidi Nurmik.

## Agency

- An AI system designed to encourage learner autonomy is not the same as a learner who
  controls the AI system.
- Questions: can I decide not to use AI, change or replace the tool, see what it
  collects, challenge its suggestions; who maintains it when its maker leaves?
- Not all learning analytics are surveillance, and local models don't guarantee privacy
  or quality. Separate student-chosen disclosure, legitimate assessment, institutional
  requirements and vendor data collection.
