# Making together

Part of `README.md`. Gathered 2026-11-11. *Speculation* and *from memory* mark what was not checked.

Software for particular people, and creativity as something a scene does.

- **Home-cooked software.** Robin Sloan, *"An app can be a home-cooked meal"* (2020): an
  app for his family of four that never had to scale.
- **Barefoot developers.** Maggie Appleton (2024): people between users and
  professionals who build for themselves and their communities
  (https://maggieappleton.com/home-cooked-software). Haav's "ICT outside IT" seen from
  below.
- **Situated software.** Clay Shirky (2004): software for one specific group.
- **Sloan, *Five Years of Home-Cooked Apps*** (https://www.robinsloan.com/lab/five-years-of-home-cooked-apps/):
  sovereignty, not just ease.

### Maggie Appleton, three talks

Appleton is a staff research engineer at GitHub Next. Her talks run from 2024 to 2026 and
track the turn from "anyone can build" to "building alone is the problem".

1. **Home-Cooked Software and Barefoot Developers** (Local-first Conference, Berlin,
   May 2024; https://maggieappleton.com/home-cooked-software).
   - Home-cooked versus industrial software. Industrial software ignores "the long tail of
     user needs".
   - Barefoot developers, named after China's 1960s barefoot doctors: technically able,
     not career programmers, embedded in a community, solving hyperlocal problems.
   - LLMs suffice for what they need: *"CRUD apps persisting data over time, with some
     basic user authentication, and a few API calls."* The gap is the glue: deploying,
     databases, auth, multiplayer.
   - Her fear: the boom happens in the cloud, so communities lose their tools to price
     rises, terms changes and ads. Hence local-first.
   - Examples: a newborn care tracker, a glucose display for a diabetic partner, a
     finance dashboard.
   - She quotes **Illich**: *"People need not only to obtain things; they need above all the
     freedom to make things among which they can live, to give shape to them according to
     their own tastes."* This links Appleton straight to convivial tools.
2. **One Developer, Two Dozen Agents, Zero Alignment** (AI Engineer, about April 2026;
   https://maggieappleton.com/zero-alignment).
   - Coding agents are built as single-player tools, but *"Software is not made by one
     person in a vacuum. It's a team sport."*
   - *"More individual output doesn't solve problems that require everyone to communicate
     and coordinate. It makes them worse."*
   - *"Implementation is rapidly becoming a solved problem … The hard question is no
     longer how to build it. It's should we build it."*
   - *"Agreeing on what to build is the new bottleneck."*
   - *"When production is cheap, opportunity cost becomes the real cost."*
   - *"Craftsmanship is what will set you apart from the vibe-coded slop."*
   - Ace, a GitHub Next prototype: a multiplayer workspace where teammates and agents
     share chat, branches, terminals and previews.
   - **This is the lonely node in industry language, said by GitHub.** Same as Anikin's
     line.
3. **Planning with Agents: Divided Worlds, Boundary Objects, and Thicker Interfaces**
   (2026; https://maggieappleton.com/planning-agents).
   - Humans live in embodiment, space, colour, gesture, voice and culture, *"all of which
     agents are terrible at understanding"*. Today's interfaces are like *"posting notes
     through a keyhole"*.
   - Boundary objects (plans, prompts, markdown) are built for the agent, not the human.
   - *"The agent can and should do 1000x more work to make the human more effective."*
     Visual, spatial, interactive and multiplayer, not walls of text.
   - Humans keep judgement, collective decisions and taste.
   - Positron's patchbay picture is a thick interface of this kind: a plan you can see and
     play.

The arc in one line: 2024, everyone can cook; 2026, everyone cooking alone is the
problem; next, tools for deciding together. It is the same arc as the Lonely Software
essay, reached from inside GitHub.

### Scenius (Brian Eno)

The communal form of genius: the intelligence and intuition of a whole scene. Eno's quote
is from memory: *"Scenius stands for the intelligence and the intuition of a whole cultural
scene. It is the communal form of the concept of the genius."* Kevin Kelly, *"Scenius, or
Communal Genius"* (2008), lists the conditions (from memory, check before a slide): mutual
appreciation, rapid exchange of tools and techniques, network effects of success, local
tolerance for novelty.

- Examples: Bauhaus, Black Mountain College, Homebrew Computer Club. Possible Estonian
  ones, unresearched: ANK '64, the Tartu painters of the 1960s–70s, early Estonian
  electronic music, the Skype founders' circle.
- Answers the lonely node: AI makes each node strong, so the scene matters more.
- A school can build the conditions: show work in progress often, let pupils take and
  change each other's work with credit (the visualia class space), swap tricks on the spot,
  leave room for strange things, hold a regular show for someone outside.
- Caveat: scenes can be cliques, and a school scene has to stay open to the quiet pupil.
- Eno's quote was confirmed by several secondary sources on 2026-11-11, for example
  https://austinkleon.com/2017/05/12/scenius/. Another wording in circulation: *"If genius
  is the creative intelligence of an individual, Scenius is the creative intelligence of a
  group."*
- **In Estonian, searched 2026-11-11: no use of "scenius" was found** (searched "scenius" on
  .ee, sirp.ee, ERR and Postimees, and the spellings "stseenius" and "kogukondlik geenius").
  Several Sirp essays on genius and community were scanned and none mention it. The term is
  effectively unintroduced in Estonian. A talk using it would be introducing it.
- The nearest Estonian trace: an art teacher's class blog (April 2026) used Austin Kleon's
  *Steal Like an Artist* with 9th graders: "varastamine" as conscious observation,
  combining influences, adding your own thought
  (https://kunstitund.wordpress.com/2026/04/01/austin-kleon-steal-like-an-artist/). Kleon
  popularised scenius in *Show Your Work!* (2014), so some teachers may already know the neighbourhood.
- Estonian words that could carry it (suggestions, not researched): *koolkond* (as in the
  Tartu painters), *seltskond*, *ring*. Glossing it as *"stseeni geenius"* or *"kogukondlik
  geenius"* works on a slide.
- **Talgud** is the Estonian cultural form closest to it (suggestion): communal work where
  everyone brings a hand and the result belongs to all. *Teeme Ära* 2008 and the *laulupidu*
  are the national-scale versions everyone knows.

### Convivial tools (Ivan Illich, 1973)

- **Convivial tools** can be used on your own terms without a specialist: the bicycle,
  the phone, the library. **Industrial tools** make you depend on them and on the
  professionals who run them.
- **Two thresholds.** A tool first helps greatly, then can turn against its users.
  **Radical monopoly**: the car-city where walking stops being an option.
- **Applied to AI:**
  - first threshold: pupils build what they never could;
  - second threshold: unaided writing becomes impossible (Suviste's point);
  - radical monopoly: all learning through one external app (TI-Hüpe risk 7).
- **A five-question test for any AI tool:**
  1. Can the pupil see what it does: model, cost, data?
  2. Can they change it?
  3. Can they do the thing without it afterwards?
  4. Can they leave and take their work?
  5. Does it connect them to people or replace them?
- The EKA course (open models on the school's machines, every call counted, the work
  belongs to the student) passes this test.
- *Deschooling Society* (1971) proposed **learning webs**: access to things, skill
  exchanges, peer matching, educators at large. That's the infrastructure of a scene.
  The book argues for replacing schools; the learning webs are the usable half.

### Distinctions

- Chosen solitude versus enforced isolation.
- Sharing as an invitation versus sharing as a requirement.
- Collaboration doesn't automatically cure loneliness, and private experiments matter.
