## Brief for the agent working on my HARNO keynote

I’m preparing a 35-minute closing keynote plus 15 minutes of Q&A for HARNO’s *Raamistikust klassiruumi* conference on 27 November 2026. The audience is around 150 Estonian teachers, school leaders and education technologists. The day introduces the updated digital competence framework. The host’s framing is that what matters is what and why we learn with technology. I want to take that into creativity, play, curiosity, and what young people will get to do for themselves as AI changes school and work. Write in English and keep it plain and speakable.

### The developer story

I remember a video where an experienced developer questioned the idea that agentic coding is like giving people power tools. The point was that the developer can end up spending the day assigning work, waiting, and checking output. Some developers got into programming because they like making things themselves. Running that production line is a different job, even when it ships more.

The closest verified source is **Michael Parker, VP of Engineering at TurinTech, on the Stack Overflow Podcast, 23 January 2026**. Parker recounts another developer who used to feel like a craftsman shaping a chair and now feels like **“a factory manager of Ikea.”** The exact line is in the transcript. The LinkedIn video is an excerpt from the same interview on a different topic. The exact video I remember and the “power tools” wording are still unverified. Use the power-saw comparison as my own framing and attribute the IKEA anecdote to the developer Parker interviewed. [Full interview and transcript](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/01/23/ai-can-10x-developers-in-creating-tech-debt/) · [LinkedIn video excerpt](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/turintechai_ai-shouldnt-treat-every-interaction-like-activity-7422250894420844546-BGiO)

Possible spoken version:

> Give a carpenter a power saw and they still get to make the chair. Put them in charge of a production line and they spend the day checking chairs.
>
> When we give a student a tool, what does it let them try for themselves? What part of the work do they now spend their time checking?

### Kristjan Port references and how they fit

- **An assignment can look finished before the student understands it.** Port describes assessment as checking whether learners can use what they know to make decisions. If an AI can supply the answer and the reasoning, ask the student what would change their answer or when it would stop being true. [Port on the illusion of learning](https://novaator.err.ee/1609708056/tehisaru-loob-oppimise-illusioonis-kaardid-sassi)
- **Understanding still takes work.** Port argues that information has become cheap while understanding still takes effort. Show the audience what that effort can feel like when it’s enjoyable. Play a short sound loop, change one parameter, and play it again. Ask what they heard. Let someone choose the next change and ask the room to predict what it will sound like. [Port on AI-generated solutions in education](https://novaator.err.ee/1609867134/tehisaru-pakutud-lahenduskaigud-muudavad-haridust-formaalseks)
- **Beginner work is also where people learn a profession.** Port uses a hypothetical example of five AI-assisted lawyers producing more contracts than ten did before. His piece is about who benefits from productivity gains. My extension is to ask what happens to the first drafts, small fixes, and other tasks that used to give beginners something to try and discuss with experienced colleagues. Present this as a question about entry routes into work. [Port on productivity and purchasing power](https://novaator.err.ee/1610186935/ti-pohine-tootlikkuse-kasv-voib-vahendada-tooliste-ostujoudu)
- **Generating ideas is one part of making something.** Port discusses a Wharton exercise in which AI-generated product ideas ranked highly for perceived purchase appeal. My *Lonely Software* question starts when something reaches another person. What do they hear or see in it, and what do they bring that changes what happens next? [Port on creativity tests](https://novaator.err.ee/1609114814/suurte-keelemudelite-voidukaik-sunnib-otsima-inimesi-uusi-loovusteste)

### Where to put this in the talk

Use the **factory-manager story before the sound demo**. The audience can experience the difference between being handed a result and getting to make a choice, hear what happened, and try something else.

Put the **beginner-work question before the ZKM story**. I was working with a SuperCollider coder and a spherical speaker. I heard something I wanted to try, and the person beside me could hear it and do something with it that I couldn’t have done alone. That brings the talk back to learning and making with another person, and to the line I want to keep:

> Something I made becomes the beginning of something else.
