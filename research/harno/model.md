### The AI criteria in the learner assessment criteria

About 60 criteria from pre-school to vocational. Mostly cautionary:

- AI errs, hallucinates, carries bias; check facts (1.2.3, 1.2.9, 1.2.13).
- Don't give AI personal data (2.2.4, 2.2.7); input may train models (2.2.8).
- Mark AI use, cite the tool (3.3.9); authorship and licence dilemmas (3.3.14–15).
- AI is a tool, not the final solution (2.4.4); not a substitute for programming (3.4.14).
- Prompts should be clear and polite (2.5.8).
- Echo chambers, polarisation, deepfake bullying (2.1.17, 2.3.9, 4.3.22).
- Energy: training and running AI costs much energy (4.4.10); avoid needless use of large
  language models (4.4 area text).

The ones pointing at making:

- 3.1.22: collaborate with AI to make more complex, creative content.
- 5.3.5: AI as a partner in the creative process.
- 5.2.6: *"teab, millal on TI kasutamine sobiv ja millal mitte"*, almost word for word
  outcome 5 of the EKA course card.
- Programming is optional in grades 7–12 (*"III ja IV kooliastmes … ei ole kohustuslik"*).


### What it covers, and what is thin

- It already includes making: content creation (3.1, with 3.1.22), programming (3.4),
  and designing solutions with technology including AI (5.3: *"disainib lahenduse mingi
  tegevuse tõhustamiseks või uuendamiseks digitehnoloogia (sh TI) abil"*). Also choosing
  whether to use AI (5.2), adapting AI to one's needs (5.2.9), evaluating one's own and
  others' work (5.3.4, per the synthesis; wording to check).
- What is thin: pupils seeing themselves as people who shape technology rather than
  comply with its rules; who the output is for (2.4 collaboration is shared documents
  and AI planning tools); cost beyond "know that it uses energy".

## The frameworks around it

- **DigComp 3.0** (European Commission, Nov 2025; publication not checked here): AI
  competences across the five areas
  (https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/scientific-activities/key-competences-lifelong-learning/digital-competence-framework-digcomp/digcomp-30_en).
- **DigCompEdu** (https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/digcompedu_en): revision
  work reportedly addresses teacher agency and professional judgement; status not checked.
- **UNESCO *AI Competency Framework for Students*** (2024): students as users and
  co-creators of AI, across human-centred thinking, ethics, techniques and system design
  (https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students).
- So "pupils as makers, not just users" is already policy. What's new is what happens
  after making becomes abundant.

## Tallinn University (read 2026-11-11)

- **"Digipädevus ja tehisaru" (HIK6442.HT)**, 6 EAP, exam; a required course for
  humanities bachelors (anthropology, Estonian and Russian philology, history,
  philosophy, cultural studies, Asian studies, European languages, PPE). Autumn 2026/27
  taught by **Andres Karjus**, in Estonian (https://ois2.tlu.ee/tluois/aine/HIK6442.HT).
  - Content: digital competence basics, data literacy and algorithmic thinking,
    quantitative and statistical methods in the humanities, principles of programming,
    data analysis and AI applications, throughout ethics, *"ökoloogilisele jalajäljele ja
    vabakasutuse võimalustele"*.
  - Outcomes: assess your own digital competence and needs; know how digital
    technologies work and their social and ecological impact, assess the footprint of
    your own use and reduce it; data types, analysis, reference managers, statistical
    thinking, bias; use AI responsibly and judge its limits, use automation and AI in
    study and research and judge how well it worked; information reliability, copyright,
    open licences.
  - Format: lectures plus one practical seminar of the student's choice in a computer
    lab (at most 30 people), with an individual assignment. Topics vary by year.
  - Close to the EKA course in two places: measuring your own footprint, and open use.
    Different in aim: literacy and research methods for humanities students, not making.
- **AI literacy competence model** (Eesti.ai with TLÜ; Linda Helene Sillat, now associate
  professor of learning technologies; updated 2026-06-22; https://eesti.ai/padevusmudel).
  For working-age people across roles and sectors. Three levels (*baastase*,
  *rakendustase*, *eksperttase*), five domains:
  1. *AI tehnoloogiad, rakendused ning digisisu loomine*
  2. *Tehisintellekti eetika ja turvalisus*
  3. *Inimkeskne mõtteviis ja suhtlus*
  4. *AI süsteemide disain*
  5. *Info- ja andmekirjaoskus*

  Each split into knowledge, skills and attitudes. Shaped like the UNESCO student
  framework (human-centred thinking, ethics, techniques, system design). Announced
  2026-07-06 (https://www.tlu.ee/dt/uudised/tallinna-ulikooli-teadlane-tootas-valja-eesti-esimese-tehisaru-kirjaoskuse-padevusmudeli).
- **Teacher in-service course** *"Tehisaru tõhus ja eetiline kasutamine õpetaja töös"*,
  3 EAP, spring 2026, taught by **Merike Saar** (on the rakkerühm), inquiry-based
  (https://www.tlu.ee/koolitused/tehisaru-tohus-ja-eetiline-kasutamine-opetaja-toos-0).
- **"Tehisintellekt õppetöös"** (2023), TLÜ's material for lecturers
  (https://oppevara.tlu.ee/node/90); the landing page only was read.
- Also: a cooperation between the National Library, TLÜ and Eesti.ai on digital and AI
  literacy (October, per search summary; not read).
