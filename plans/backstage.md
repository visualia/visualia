# Backstage: what may be inferred about a student's process

A standing rule (2026-11-02), for every feature that records or reads a
student's work in progress: boards, versions, nudges, taste rules
(`taste-rules.md`) and whatever comes later. The reasoning is in
`schools/eka/docs/DRIFT.md` ("The boundary").

Recording a student's process drifted, idea by idea, into monitoring once
already, in the chat these plans came from. The trouble is not logging (Figma,
Docs and git keep everything) but where it flows, what is read into it, and a
backstage that stops being safe for bad ideas.

## The rule

- **Logging stays** as now: undo, versions, recovery.
- **Inference runs only when the student asks**, for the student. No
  background jobs read process.
- **The backstage is private by default** (unfinished work, failed attempts).
  Showing anything to the class or the teacher is an explicit publish step.
- **Nothing interpretive is kept** unless the student keeps it.
- **No teacher dashboard of process**, no grading of process, no class
  comparison of process. Only works students choose to show.

## Grounds

- Helen Nissenbaum's contextual integrity: the same version history is fine
  for you and your collaborators, and different when it flows to the person who
  grades you.
- Erving Goffman's front and back stage: an analysed backstage invites
  performance and narrows exploration.
- The TI-Hüpe risk analysis (University of Tartu Centre for Ethics, 2026) asks
  whether learning becomes excessively monitored and datafied.

## Outside it

Untouched by this rule, since none of them read process: small-data training,
Judgement Machines on submitted works, archive work. The rule itself is course
material: students can design what may be recorded, by whom, for whom.
